# Phase 3: does training for longer blocks / a recursive second pass beat chain and tree b16? (Qwen3-8B, A100-80GB, SGLang 0.5.22)

Short answer: **no.** With a warm-start fine-tune (the only training affordable under the cap), a drafter trained at block 32 is no better than the *untrained* b32 run of the official b16 checkpoint, and the best-first tree on the official b16 weights (Phase 2) remains the best configuration. A recursive pass 2 trained on pass-1 drafts reaches the quality of a single-pass trained b32 (0.994x) and no more, so with its extra draft pass it cannot beat the Phase 2 tree. Recursive drafting was killed offline; it was not integrated into the engine.

## What was run
- Data (`train/prep_prompts.py`, `train/gen_responses.py`): 45K prompts from `mlabonne/open-perfectblend` (the DSpine README's mix; MetaMath 25%, UltraInteract 18%, UltraChat 16%, orca-math 15%, evol-codealpaca 8%, ...), first human turn, dedup, **dropped 1,262 prompts with any 8-gram overlap or exact match with the full GSM8K / MATH-500 / HumanEval(+solutions) / MBPP / MT-Bench test sets**; ~50% of math prompts get the eval's "\boxed" suffix (format only). Responses regenerated by Qwen3-8B itself, thinking OFF, T=0.7 top_p 0.8 top_k 20, max 1536 new tokens, SGLang AR. First 30,000 completed samples used (mean 550 response tokens, 6% truncated); last 256 held out.
- Trainer (`train/train_dspine.py`): SpecForge `main` DSpine draft model + objective with PR #1038 (loss normalisation) applied, but a single-GPU in-process HF Qwen3-8B target instead of SpecForge's Mooncake/SGLang-capture plane (colocated, bf16 params + fp32 master AdamW). Checkpoints are converted to the Tencent/SGLang `selected_prefix_bridge.*` format and load in the DSPINE plugin unchanged (verified: official weights round-trip through the trainer; a 20-step pilot served at tau 10.5 on GSM8K). Loss is divided by the number of supervised rows (SpecForge returns a per-sequence sum; with clip 1.0 that scale matters).
- **From-scratch training was not affordable** and was not run: measured 0.56 s per sequence at 128 anchors on A100 (the paper's 512 anchors x ~68K sequences x 6 epochs is ~100x our budget). The pilot (from scratch) hit 44 s/step at 512 anchors. Instead both arms are **warm starts from the official `tencent/Qwen3-8B-DSpine-b16`** (same data, same 925 optimizer steps x 32 sequences x 64 anchors/sequence, LR 2e-4 cosine, 4% warmup, correct-predecessor curriculum off):
  - `ft16`: block 16 (control: what does our data/recipe do on its own?)
  - `ft32`: block 32 (new positions 16..31 start from the b16 extrapolation).
  So this answers "does training positions 16-31 help over free extrapolation", not "does a from-scratch b32 beat a from-scratch b16".
- Eval: the Phase 1/2 harness unchanged (`bench/run_cell.py`, 5 datasets x 40 prompts, T=0, c=1, max_new 2048, thinking off, same prompt files), script `bench/p3_eval.sh`, summary `bench/p3_summarize.py` -> `bench/results/p3_summary.json`. Tree = the Phase 2 in-engine best-first tree (N=48 nodes, depth cap D). x AR uses the Phase 1 AR cells per dataset, like Phase 1/2. The Phase 2 controls were re-run on the fresh A100 and reproduce Phase 2 (b16 523 tok/s, b32 557, tree48 623 vs 524/558/627); a second A100 box gave 523 again for b16.

## Results (T=0, c=1, mean of 5 datasets)
| config | tau | tok/s | x AR | vs official b16 chain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| official b16, chain | 7.99 | 523 | 5.78x | 1.000 |
| official b16 weights, chain b32 (untrained extrapolation) | 9.03 | 557 | 6.15x | 1.065 |
| official b16 weights, tree N=48 D=31 (Phase 2 winner) | 11.29 | 623 | 6.88x | 1.191 |
| our ft16 (b16-trained control), chain b16 | 8.00 | 523 | 5.78x | 1.000 |
| our ft16 weights, chain b32 | 8.94 | 553 | 6.11x | 1.058 |
| our ft16 weights, tree N=48 D=15 | 9.87 | 559 | 6.18x | 1.070 |
| our ft16 weights, tree N=48 D=31 | 11.26 | 630 | 6.95x | 1.204 |
| **our ft32 (b32-trained), chain b32** | **8.90** | **548** | **6.05x** | **1.048** |
| **our ft32, tree N=48 D=31** | **11.15** | **622** | **6.87x** | **1.190** |

Per dataset (tau / tok/s) are in `bench/results/p3_summary.json`; raw cells `bench/results/p3_<tag>_<ds>_T0_c1.json`, logs `logs/p3/`. Run-to-run noise is about 1-2%. (b64 was not trained: b32 showed no gain, which was the precondition.)

Reading:
- The b16 control confirms that our data/recipe neither helps nor hurts the official checkpoint (8.00 vs 7.99): its own training distribution was already well matched, and ~1 epoch of 30K samples at low LR is a tiny perturbation.
- Training positions 16-31 for 925 steps gave **8.90 vs 9.03** (untrained extrapolation) in chain mode and **11.15 vs 11.29** with the tree, i.e. slightly worse, within noise. The extrapolated positions of the official checkpoint are already about as good as this much training can make them; the long-range conditional acceptance is limited by the information in the parallel/MASK formulation, not by lack of training at those positions (teacher-forced: ft32's first 15 positions give tau 8.49 vs 8.47 for ft16, and all 31 give 10.18).
- The tree gain is what the gain was in Phase 2 (+19-20%); our retrained weights neither add to nor subtract from it (ft16 + tree 1.204, ft32 + tree 1.190, official 1.191).
- Caveat on strength: this is 1/200 of the compute behind a from-scratch DSpine, so it cannot rule out that a drafter *pre-trained* from scratch at long blocks with a large budget (DBloom's claim) does better. It does show that cheap adaptation of the official b16 checkpoint to b32 buys nothing over a config change.

## T2: recursive second pass (kill test, `train/recursive_pass2.py`, `logs/p3/recursive_pass2_train.log`)
Design: pass 1 = frozen ft16 (b16, MASK inputs, greedy backbone tokens d_1..d_15). Pass 2 = trainable copy of the ft32 weights whose block input is `[anchor, d_1..d_15, MASK x 16]` (pass-1 draft *tokens* instead of MASKs), emitting 31 tokens. Trained on-policy w.r.t. pass 1 (the d_i are the frozen model's own drafts for the same anchors/contexts), 300 steps x 32 sequences x 64 anchors, same objective, LR 1e-4. Metric: teacher-forced greedy chain acceptance length (bonus counted) on 256 held-out sequences x 128 random anchors, identical anchors for every variant; this is a proxy (engine tau/tok/s for the same weights differ, e.g. ft32 chain: proxy 10.2 for 31 draft tokens vs 8.90 in the engine), used only for ratios.

| step | tau pass 1 (b16) | tau pass 2 (31 tok) | pass 2 / pass 1 | single-pass trained b32 | pass 2 / b32 | pass 2 truncated to 15 tok |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (untrained, tokens fed to a MASK-trained model) | 8.47 | 2.73 | 0.32 | 10.18 | 0.27 | 2.73 |
| 100 | 8.47 | 9.69 | 1.144 | 10.18 | 0.951 | 8.08 |
| 200 | 8.47 | 10.05 | 1.187 | 10.18 | 0.987 | 8.25 |
| 300 | 8.47 | 10.12 | 1.195 | 10.18 | 0.994 | 8.29 |

- Pass 2 / pass 1 = 1.195 clears the plan's 1.05 gate, but **only because it is longer**: the single-pass b32 model gets 10.18 (1.20x) without any second pass, and pass 2 never exceeds it (0.994x after 300 steps). On equal length (first 15 positions) pass 2 is 8.29 vs 8.49 for single-pass b32 and 8.47 for pass 1: conditioning on pass-1 tokens gives no refinement gain on top of what DSpine's in-block predecessor injection and serial refinement already provide (consistent with the Phase 2 argument).
- Economics: the Phase 2 chain b32 (single pass, untrained) already gives +6.5% tok/s for tau 9.03; pass 2 would add a second draft pass (~3 ms on a ~14-15 ms round, +20%) for a tau no higher than a single-pass b32. The tree (627 tok/s) needs a tau of ~11.1 from a chain at 17.7 ms/round to be matched; the pass-2 proxy ratio over single-pass b32 is <= 1.0. **Net: the recursive pass-2 variant loses to chain b32 and to the b16 tree.** It was killed at the offline test; no engine integration or tok/s measurement was done (hence no "pass-2" row in the table above). Not tried: a pass 2 that outputs a tree, a smaller/cheaper pass-2 head, pass-2 training from scratch with many more steps, feeding pass-1 *features* rather than tokens (dspine.md idea 7), more on-policy rounds. I would not expect these to change the sign given pass 2 only ties single-pass b32 in chain mode.

## Reproduction
Prompts/regen: `train/prep_prompts.py`, `train/gen_responses.py`; training: `python train/train_dspine.py --data regen_final.jsonl --init-official tencent/Qwen3-8B-DSpine-b16 --block {16,32} --anchors 64 --lr 2e-4 --replace-prob 0 --epochs 1 --micro-bs 2 --accum 16` (needs SpecForge main + PR #1038 and the trainer's `train/dspine_sf_config.json`); eval `bench/p3_eval.sh TAG DIR BLOCK [N:D]`; T2 `train/recursive_pass2.py`. Environment gotchas on a fresh box: flashinfer JIT needs `nvidia-nvvm`/`nvidia-cuda-crt` pinned to 13.0.* beside `nvidia-cuda-nvcc==13.0.*` (else PTX 9.5 vs ptxas 9.0) and `CUDA_HOME` pointing at the pip cu13 dir with `lib64`/`libcudart.so` symlinks.
